### B. Verdicts

#### France

**F1. Normalize French inputs before C2 rescoring [C1, C3] → ADOPT (first GPU job).**
- C2 scores ~0.0003 for "Atelier Morel" vs "Atelier Morel SARL" at the same address; that class is 99.85–99.99% true
  in US/India [M].
- Cie/services/associés/et fils behave as copy suffixes (house-number-shift rate 0.00–0.06) [M].
- **Guard [M, `diagnostics/20260927_reset/F/siblings/sibling_report.json`]:** 6.0% of French S1s share core name,
  house number and city with another S1 and differ only by street and legal form (US/India: 0.002–0.005%). Example:
  "Bordeaux Agricole SARL, 17 Rue Tillet" vs "Bordeaux Agricole SAS, 17 Rue de Dijon".
- **So:**
  - strip only legal forms and copy suffixes;
  - never normalize street names;
  - keep the legal-form relation as a separate decision feature.

**F2. Rescore every French pair, not a US-defined band [C5] → ADOPT.**
- The misfiring class sits at C2 ≈ 0.0003, below every band we used.

  contaminated.

**F4. Probe truth rates as French anchors [C5] → ADOPT.**

## 1. Problem, metric and score arithmetic

- **Task:** for each Source-1 business, list (≤ 11) the S2/S3 record IDs of the same business. Each record has at most
  one owner. Test: 1,732,544 S1s (US 38%, India 47%, France 15%, unlabelled).
- **Metric:** macro F0.5 per S1. An empty list on a true no-match S1 scores 1; any link on it scores 0.
- **Arithmetic:** public = 0.850248·US/India + 0.149752·France. A perfect France with today's US/India gives 0.98775.

## 3. Research: what the winners did (sources in the research log)

**Foursquare Location Matching 2022**, the closest analogue (1.5M noisy multilingual business records):
- 1st place pipeline:
  - 200 candidates per id, pruned to ~40 by a small LightGBM (max IoU 0.979);
  - a LightGBM on ~120 features (Levenshtein, Jaro-Winkler, **per-id max/min/mean and ratios**);
  - xlm-roberta-large and mdeberta **with ~70–90 numeric features concatenated to the head** (+0.03 CV over CatBoost);
  - set-merge post-processing (+0.0056).
- Other places:
  - 2nd: a "transformer blocker" over the whole candidate set; recall 0.9895.
  - 7th and 13th: graph/2-hop reasoning lifted the recall ceiling 0.978 → 0.9935 and 0.971 → 0.993; a GNN gave +0.02.
  - 15th: clustering +0.025.
- Multilingual handling: romanized/ASCII views and character TF-IDF; translation mostly didn't help.
- The final leaderboard was decided by a train/test overlap leak, **which we measured as absent here.**

**Shopee Price Match** (per-item mean F1, close to our metric):
- **set-size and metric-aware decoding**: min-2 matches +0.019; set-size prior +0.02; target cluster size +0.009;
- a second-stage GBDT on 500 features (+0.02);
- neighbourhood blending.

**Quora and Santander:** frequency and graph "magic" features computed over train + test. **Instacart:** per-order
expected-F with an explicit "None" model (+0.007).

**Jigsaw Multilingual:** pseudo-labels on the unlabelled languages were essential (0.937 → 0.954). But France research
notes that self-training cannot fix *systematic* misses such as "X" vs "X SARL", so normalization and synthetic data come
first.

**Entity-matching literature:**
- Fine-tuned LLMs generalize better to unseen distributions than BERT-class models (Peeters et al.; Steiner et al.
  2024). Error-based example selection +5 F1.
- Letting the model see competing candidates beats independent pairs (ComEM, +16% F1).
- Qwen3-Reranker-4B: Apache, 4.0B; MMTEB-R 72.7 vs 66.4 (0.6B) and 58.4 (bge-m3). The 8B is 8.2B, over the cap.

**Decoding theory for our metric** (GFM; Ye et al. 2012) [R + I]:
- The *first* link pays when p > P(no match), ≈ 0.5 for a lone candidate.
- The 2nd link needs p > 0.73, the 3rd > 0.76, rising toward 0.8.
- **Our single global cut (0.725) therefore empties S1s whose only plausible candidate sits at 0.5–0.72.** That is
  relevant to the empty-address bucket.
- Our earlier expected-F attempt failed on *uncalibrated* scores (−0.0045); it needs calibration plus a no-match model.

**Dream-RSI** (Google/DeepMind + UMD, arXiv 2609.14858, Sep 2026). Reviewed at the user's request.

**What it does:**
- It improves an **LLM coding agent's exploration policy**: which branches to expand, in what order, how much in
  parallel, and when to stop, in propose→evaluate discovery loops.
- Recorded discovery trees serve as a "replay simulator". Thousands of alternative policies are scored offline
  ("dreaming") on stored outcomes, and the best is redeployed. The underlying model is never trained, and no new
  solutions are generated during replay.
- **Results:** 1.4–2.4× fewer attempts, or up to 2.1× better results, on algorithm, maths and GPU-kernel tasks, with
  hundreds to thousands of scored attempts per round.

**Fit for us:**
- **Not directly applicable.**
  - Our task is supervised matching with a deadline. We will run tens of experiments, not hundreds of cheap scored
    attempts, so a replay simulator would have too little history.
  - France has no automatic evaluator (5 uploads per day).
  - The paper also warns that replay covers only the part of the space already explored.
- **Two ideas we already apply, now stated explicitly:**
  - **(i) Cached scores as the simulator.** Every decision policy (P5 decoding, P2 pruning cut-offs, thresholds) is
    searched offline over cached arena scores before anything is deployed.
  - **(ii) The synthetic French world as France's simulator.** French policies are chosen there before spending a
    real upload. Its coverage risk is exactly the paper's caveat, hence the TSTR/adversarial validation in P6.
- **One finding we adopt:** turning history into explicit instructions narrowed exploration in their experiments. We
  keep agent briefs factual rather than prescriptive about solutions.

## 4. Strategy: six pillars (what, why, expected effect, risk)

### P1. Normalization and generator inversion (enables P2, P3, P4 and P6)

**Canonical views**, computed for every record and S1:
- folded (accents, case, punctuation);
- **legal-form-stripped**, plus a legal-form family feature (same / added / compatible / conflicting);
- **transliterated**: an Indic→Latin word dictionary learned from train pairs, with anyascii (ISC) fallback;
- **French canonical**:
  - street types (AFNOR/La Poste abbreviations);
  - bis/ter;
  - N°/No/# formats;
  - département→région (full table);
  - Cie/Compagnie, Ets, St/Ste;
- DBA/aka/née/formerly split;
- acronym and compact (domain-style) forms.

**Generator inversion:**
- **(a) Typo repair:** project record tokens onto the same-country S1 vocabulary (edit distance ≤ 2, frequency-ranked).
  The generator applies single-character typos to vocabulary words, so this directly undoes them.
- **(b) "Explained by copy operations" test:** can the record be reached from the S1 using only measured *copy*
  operations (legal-form change, abbreviation, accent, département↔région, bis/ter, ≤ k typos) rather than *decoy*
  operations (changed number, swapped template word)? Emitted as a feature.
- **(c) Name informativeness:** −log frequency of the name template, over S1s and the pool, train + test.

**Why:** abbreviations and French conventions are the documented weak spot of learned matchers [R]; domain
normalization gave +1–6 F1 in Ditto [R]; other participants built exactly this [R].

**Effect [I]:** +0.001–0.002 US/India, via P2 and P3; the main driver for France. **Risk:** over-normalizing away
evidence that distinguishes decoys. Mitigate by keeping raw views alongside and emitting features rather than
overwriting text.

### P2. Recall: multi-route candidates, reverse retrieval, 2-hop, learned pruning

**Candidate routes (union):**
- dense S1→record top-50 (existing);
- **reverse record→S1 top-5–10** (owners crowded out of templated S1 lists);
- character 3–4-gram TF-IDF on normalized names;
- exact keys: canonical name; house number + street + city; acronym; compact name; transliterated skeleton;
- the existing address and empty-address branches;
- **2-hop:** records similar to the S1's confident records (aliases, native-script names, empty-address copies are
  reachable through siblings).

**Pruning:** a light LightGBM on route/rank features cuts to ~25–30 per S1. **Target:** best-possible score ≥ 0.997 on
B_REST.

**The arena must be extended with the same routes** (A for training, B_REST for gating).

**Why:** retrieval is ~50% of US/India loss [M]; winners' ceilings were 0.979–0.994, and 2-hop raised them to 0.993
[R].

**Effect [I]:** +0.002–0.004. **Risk:** decoy flood and compute. Mitigate with the pruner plus route and rank features.

### P3. Rich GBDT decision model (the core)

**~150–250 features per pair:**
- **Similarities:** RapidFuzz ratio, partial, token-set, token-sort, WRatio and Jaro-Winkler; Levenshtein; char-3gram
  TF-IDF cosine. Each over 4–5 views, for name, street, city and full address.
- **Structure:** containment P/R; house-number and postcode relations; legal-form family relation; acronym, domain,
  alias and bridge flags; empty and native-script flags; **record source S2/S3**; route flags.
- **Model scores:** P1 features; C2, Qwen-0.6B and (P4) 4B scores.
- **Listwise, per S1:** rank, gap to best, z-score, ratio to max for the key scores.
- **Competition, per record:** best other S1 score, margin, reverse rank, claimant count.
- **Sibling agreement:** similarity to the S1's confident records.
- **Frequency:** term-frequency-adjusted name and address counts over train + test.
- The existing collective features.

**Training: corrected after the OOF audit.** The earlier draft trained this model on A *with* C2/Qwen/4B scores. Those
scores are **in-sample on A** (the rerankers were trained on A), so the GBDT would learn to over-trust them. Out-of-fold
prediction at the GBDT level does not fix that. The corrected protocol:
1. **Freeze one fold file for B_REST:** `fold = hash(salt, group) % 5`.
   - A group is B_REST S1s joined through shared records with any score ≥ 0.05.
   - Recompute the groups when P2 adds routes; the largest group must stay under 0.5% of B_REST.
   - Every variant uses this same file, so comparisons stay paired.
2. **Level 1 (optional), on A.** A rich GBDT on ~500k component-grouped A S1s using only features that are
   out-of-sample on A:
   - text and structure features;
   - v1 tree/blend;
   - collective features;
   - listwise features built from those.

   **Never C2/Qwen/4B.** A 10–20% A holdout is used for early stopping. It predicts B_REST, SLICE and test.
3. **Level 2, on B_REST with 5-fold OOF.**
   - Inputs: the level-1 probability; C2, Qwen and 4B scores; their listwise, rival and agreement features.
   - Fixed hyperparameters; no inner fits (thresholds are chosen cross-fitted on OOF).
   - Features built from level-2 predictions come only from OOF.
4. **Test:** level 1 from A, then **the mean of the 5 level-2 fold models**, so that the OOF-fitted calibration and decode
   settings apply to exactly the predictor that was evaluated. The earlier practice was a single 3M-row refit.
5. **Frequency and count features are normalized per country size.** The arena has 1.9× more US S1s than the test
   (1.24M vs 0.66M).

**Gate:**
- component bootstrap against the fixed 0.985511 per-S1 vector;
- the finalist is rerun with a second fold salt;
- CI above 0 in both runs.

**Why:** our decision model has only ~20 features [M] against 120–500 for winners [R]; listwise and competition features
target the shared-name empty-address bucket [R, ComEM].

**Effect [I]:** +0.001–0.003. **Risk:** feature compute at scale (~50M pairs). Mitigate by computing B_REST and test
first, sampling A, and vectorizing with RapidFuzz `cdist`/`cpdist` on EC2.

### P4. Stronger pair models: a cascade, fed into P3

- **(a) Faster trainer first (enabler):** BF16, packing, sorted buckets. Our 0.6B trainer runs at ~10% of GPU peak [M].
- **(b) Qwen-0.6B retrained on 2–5M error-focused pairs,** built from P2 candidates and P1 views, plus synthetic French.
  It is 4–8× faster than 4B [R].
- **(c) Qwen3-Reranker-4B LoRA** (r 32–64, lr 1–2e-4, 1 epoch, 3–8 hard negatives per positive plus random), used
  **only on the uncertain band** (2–4M pairs). Implementation details:
  - one-logit head;
  - S1 in the query slot so prefix caching applies;
  - pre-tokenized input.

  Expected: ~1.5–2.5 h to train 1M pairs, ~1.5–3 h to score [R].
- **Why:** Qwen-0.6B matched C2 on 12× less data [M]; LLMs are more robust out of distribution, which matters for France
  [R].
- **Effect [I]:** +0.0005–0.002 US/India; potentially large for France.
- **Gate:** a 45-minute 4B screen against 0.6B on B_REST before the full run.

### P5. Metric-aware decoding

**Recipe:**
1. Isotonic calibration, out-of-fold, per country.
2. An **S1-level count model** (0 / 1 / 2 / 3+, including "has a match that wasn't retrieved").
3. **Per-S1 expected-F0.5 over k = 0..11** top candidates.
4. Greedy gain-based one-owner resolution.
5. One guard scalar tuned on B_REST.

**Why:** the theory says our global cut is wrong for lone candidates [R]; the Instacart None model and Shopee set-size
priors gave +0.007 and +0.02 [R].

**Quick win, available now [V, OOF audit].**
- `ber/v2_decision.py` already implements cross-fitted per-country isotonic calibration and a count posterior. Its folds
  are S1-level halves, which must be switched to component groups.
- The saved OOF files (`/opt/az-ml-new/agents/pair_tree_c2/step040000/oof.npy` and the Qwen ensemble OOF) allow **P5 to
  be tested on B_REST with no new model fits**, in about 1 CPU-hour.

**What the saved OOF shows:**
- It is nearly calibrated overall: sum of probabilities 1,768,994 against 1,769,048 positives.
- US is over-confident in the middle band (predicted 0.706, observed 0.679); India is under-confident (0.288 vs 0.325).
  This is exactly what per-country isotonic calibration fixes.
- Unretrieved true links average 0.0725 per B_REST S1; that is the count model's "missing" mass.

**Effect:** every decoding change measured so far is ≤ +0.0004 [M]; expect ≤ +0.0004. **Risk:** calibration drift,
especially in France. Mitigate with French calibration on the
synthetic check set.

### P6. France program

**Order:**
1. **Sibling check.** Are there French S1s with the same core name at the same address? It decides whether legal forms
   may be canonicalized.
2. **Canonical inputs.** P1 views fed to C2, then a rescore of French pairs (20 min on the GPU, no training).
3. **Synthetic French data** from French test S1 text (approved, disclosed), then validation:
   - train-on-synthetic / test-on-real on US/India (TSTR);
   - adversarial validation;
   - positive-aware hard negatives.
4. **Training.** French-aware 0.6B/4B with ≥ 50% real replay plus WiSE-FT, to protect US/India.
5. **One guarded co-training round.** Accept a pseudo-label only when two models agree, the pair passes the copy-ops
   test, and uncertainty is low.
6. **French calibration and decoding** on the synthetic check set plus the count prior.

**Measurement:** France is measured only by upload subtraction, at most 3–5 times [R warns against leaderboard
overfitting]: France = (public − 0.850248 × arena-predicted US/India) / 0.149752.

**Effect [I]:** France 0.937 → 0.96–0.975 (research estimate) = +0.0035–0.0057 overall. **Risk:** there is no French
label to validate against, and the generator may diverge from the real one.

## 5. The arena: what it is, validity, flaws

- **What:** all 2,072,821 eligible train S1s × dense top-50 over the full train pool: 103.6M labelled pairs. HOLDOUT-2 is
  removed.
- **Partitions:** A (1,450,975; training), B_REST (521,846; evaluation, cross-fit by component halves), SLICE (100,000;
  never read). They are split by claim-graph components, so competing S1s mostly stay together.
- **Validity:** B_REST predicted test US/India within ~±0.0005 after country-mix adjustment [M + I], assuming France's
  no-match rate equals train's.

**Flaws, and fixes:**
1. **No France.** Use the synthetic check set plus upload subtraction.
2. **Dense-only candidates.** Extend the arena with the P2 routes before judging them.
3. **Fewer decoys (4.67 vs 5.8 records per S1).** Re-check any rule sensitive to unclaimed records with the withheld-S1
   simulation.
4. **Components use a score cut.** Low-score cross-partition contests are ignored; minor, and validated by the transfer
   result.
5. **B_REST reused across many selections.** Train on A only, use cross-fit plus CI, and **read SLICE once** as the final
   pre-upload confirmation.
