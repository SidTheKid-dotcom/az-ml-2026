# Moving an entity matcher to France without French labels: transfer, label-free ensembling and threshold setting

Context: three candidate matchers can score French pairs. (a) A string-feature GBDT whose mean scores already match the known truth rates on two probe subsets (≈0.67 vs 64–69% true; ≈0.93 vs 92–96% true). (b) bge-reranker-v2-m3 fine-tuned on "Frenchified" train pairs. (c) A Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B variant. The original cross-encoder misfires on France, for example scoring "X" vs "X SARL" at 0.0003. French precision is ≈0.97 and recall ≈0.82–0.92. No French labels exist, and no training data may be built from test text. The compute budget is about 90 minutes on existing scores.

## Q1. Which domain-adaptation techniques for entity matching are proven (DADER, adversarial training, feature alignment, mixup), and what gains do they report?

### Takeaway
Published domain adaptation for deep entity matching (DADER and later work) sometimes gives large F1 gains when the source-trained model does badly on the target. The results are highly unstable, though: across source→target pairs the same method can gain or lose tens of F1 points. The reported setups also use target-domain validation labels for tuning or early stopping. With no French labels and 90 minutes of compute, feature-alignment domain adaptation is a poor bet. Better bets are cheap target-shaped data augmentation (the "Frenchify" approach), adding a language-agnostic feature model, and label-free calibration.

### Cited Findings
- DADER (Tu et al., SIGMOD 2022) splits domain adaptation for entity resolution into three parts: a Feature Extractor, a Matcher and a Feature Aligner. It implements six aligners: MMD and K-order (discrepancy-based), GRL, InvGAN and InvGAN+KD (adversarial), and ED (reconstruction) — [DADER GitHub](https://github.com/ruc-datalab/DADER); [SIGMOD paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3514221.3517870)
- DADER's own summary: "discrepancy-based methods (e.g., MMD) are more stable but adversarial-based method InvGAN+KD works the best when carefully tuning hyper-parameters". Also: "DA can significantly improve ER when the model trained on the source does not perform well on the target", "Choosing a 'close' source domain for DA is more beneficial", "Not all successful DA methods in CV and NLP are suitable to ER", "Pre-trained LM Feature Extractor works better than RNN based methods", and feature-level domain adaptation beats instance-based — [DADER GitHub](https://github.com/ruc-datalab/DADER)
- The DADER demo reaches F1 0.86 on iTunes-Amazon with zero target labels. Labelling only 3 pairs out of a 50-pair active-learning sample raised it to 0.90 — [DADER VLDB demo](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p3666-fan.pdf)
- The DADER package API takes target validation labels (`aligner.fit(X_src, y_src, X_tgt, X_tgt_val, y_tgt_val, ...)`), with `valid_rate = 0.1` of the target set used for validation. So the "zero-label" pipeline as published still monitors target F1 during training — [dader on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dader/); the demo also "draw[s] the F1-score curve of target validation set during training" — [DADER VLDB demo](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p3666-fan.pdf)
- An independent re-run of DADER methods (the MFSN paper) shows the instability. On similar-domain task DZY→FZ, NoDA scored 82.87 F1 and DADER-MMD scored 66.98, so MMD lost about 16 points. On RI→WA1, NoDA scored 49.51 and MMD scored 24.51. On FZ→DZY, NoDA scored 6.27 and MMD 53.19. Averaged over all tasks: NoDA 49.52; DADER MMD 54.49, CORAL 60.83, GRL 58.05, InvGAN 57.49, InvGAN+KD 58.09. The authors' MFSN-FRSE-MMD reached 67.86 — [MFSN paper, OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=1GVyE9J021)
- The same study found DADER's ED (reconstruction) "even worse than NoDA in most cases" — [MFSN paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=1GVyE9J021)
- Low-resource deep entity resolution with dataset adaptation through a gradient-reversal layer, combined with active learning, is an earlier approach (Kasai et al., ACL 2019) — [ACL Anthology P19-1586](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1586.pdf)
- Self-training (pseudo-labelling) is an alternative to feature alignment. Under distribution shift, however, "pseudo-labels can be unreliable in terms of their large discrepancy from target ground truth", and entropy- or confidence-based selection criteria lose accuracy sharply under domain shift — [Cycle Self-Training, NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/c1fea270c48e8079d8ddf7d06d26ab52-Paper.pdf)
- Self-training for entity alignment picks up false-positive noise from high-confidence wrong predictions. One-to-one and mutual-nearest-neighbour constraints on pseudo-labels (BootEA, MRAEA, STEA) reduce that noise — [STEA, arXiv 2211.16101](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16101)
- Theory: "producing an optimal predictor using only labeled source and unlabeled target data is well-known to be impossible absent further assumptions" — [Garg et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234)

### Inferences
- The CE misfire ("X" vs "X SARL" → 0.0003) looks like a gap in the training data (it never saw legal-form suffixes as non-distinguishing tokens), not a feature-distribution shift that MMD or GRL alignment would fix. Targeted augmentation, which is what Frenchify already does, attacks the cause directly. DADER-style alignment does not guarantee that a specific token becomes invariant.
- Without French labels there is no safe way to early-stop or choose the domain-adaptation aligner. Given the ±16–25 F1 swings shown above, unlabelled adversarial or MMD training is a gamble. The most defensible gain is to **self-train on French target pairs only where independent models agree strongly** (GBDT and Frenchified CE both confident), with a one-to-one constraint per S1. Even this should count only as a hypothesis until a proxy check confirms it (see Q3).

#### Ranked techniques for this team (inference; ranks combine evidence strength with fit to the 90-minute, existing-scores budget)
1. **Calibrate every model on France using the two probe subsets as anchors, then set thresholds by expected-F0.5 optimisation over calibrated probabilities.** Zero training, fits the budget (Q3).
2. **GBDT-anchored ensemble.** Average calibrated probabilities, or use a GBDT veto or rescue on disagreements. The GBDT is the only model with verified calibration on France. It is also language-agnostic, so its errors should correlate less with the CE and Qwen errors (Q2).
3. **Estimate the French link prevalence with quantification methods**: EM/SLD on calibrated GBDT outputs, Median Sweep/TSMax adjusted counts, or a GMM fit ZeroER-style. Use it as a count target and a sanity check on every threshold (Q3).
4. **Frenchify-style augmentation (already in hand), extended.** This is the entity-matching analogue of adding target-like training pairs. Peeters & Bizer found that mixing languages always helped (Q4).
5. **Agreement-filtered self-training on French pairs.** Needs GPU time and carries a confirmation-bias risk; try it only if items 1–3 leave a measurable recall gap.
6. **DADER-style MMD or InvGAN+KD alignment.** Last. Unstable, and it needs target labels for model selection.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the original DADER SIGMOD 2022 table with exact per-task gains. The MFSN re-run is the only per-task numbers found, and it is a third-party (OpenReview, apparently under review) paper.
- No paper found applies DADER-style domain adaptation to a **cross-lingual or cross-country business-name** target. All reported tasks are English benchmark-to-benchmark transfers such as product, citation and restaurant data.
- I did not verify Ditto's MixDA augmentation gains or any cross-domain evidence for them in this session (the search was rate-limited).

## Q2. How should several models be combined on an unlabelled domain (majority vote, rank averaging, agreement-based selection, Dawid–Skene/Snorkel label models, unsupervised calibration), and which works best for matching?

### Takeaway
With only three models, two of them transformers trained on the same US/India pairs, simple aggregation (majority or soft vote on calibrated scores) is the robust default. Benchmark evidence shows majority vote is among the most consistent aggregators. Label models such as Dawid–Skene and spectral methods need conditional independence and at least three sources, and the CE and Qwen models break that assumption. The best use of label-model ideas here is diagnostic: estimate how accurate each model is on France from agreement patterns, and do not trust the learned weights blindly.

### Cited Findings
- The WRENCH weak-supervision benchmark (22 datasets, more than 120 method variants) compares Majority Vote (MV), Weighted MV, Dawid–Skene, Data Programming (Snorkel), MeTaL and FlyingSquid — [WRENCH, NeurIPS 2021 D&B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11377)
- A 2024 follow-up benchmark uses "majority vote (MV) as the default LM ... following WRENCH's takeaway that MV is one of the most consistent methods across various tasks". COSINE and ARS2 "did not ... consistently improve[] upon MV" — [Stronger Than You Think, NeurIPS 2024 D&B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.07727)
- Spectral Meta-Learner (Parisi et al., PNAS 2014): if classifiers are conditionally independent given the label, the off-diagonal of their covariance matrix is rank-one. The entries of its leading eigenvector are proportional to each classifier's balanced accuracy, so you can rank classifiers and weight an ensemble **without labels** — [Parisi et al., arXiv 1303.3257](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3257)
- Platanios et al. (ICML 2016) estimate classifier accuracy from agreement patterns on unlabelled data alone, getting "within a few percent of the true accuracy" on two real data sets. They also combine classifiers using the estimated accuracies — [Platanios, Dubey, Mitchell, ICML 2016](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/platanios16.html); a later version reports this on four data sets — [arXiv 1705.07086](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07086)
- Agreement-on-the-line: when accuracy-on-the-line holds, out-of-distribution agreement between pairs of neural networks is linearly correlated with in-distribution agreement, with the same slope and bias. This lets you predict out-of-distribution accuracy without labels. The pattern "appears to only hold for neural network classifiers" — [Baek et al., arXiv 2206.13089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13089)
- Panda brings Snorkel's labelling-function and label-model abstraction specifically to entity matching, with EM-specific tooling and automatically generated labelling functions — [Panda demo, arXiv 2106.10821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10821)
- ZeroER shows the match and non-match distributions of **similarity feature vectors** can be learned with a Gaussian mixture model plus adaptive regularisation and a transitivity constraint. On five benchmarks it "greatly outperforms existing unsupervised approaches and achieves comparable performance to supervised approaches" — [ZeroER, SIGMOD 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06049); code under Apache-2.0 — [GitHub](https://github.com/chu-data-lab/zeroer)
- In entity-alignment self-training, "parallel pseudo-label ensembling" (consensus across independently trained models) combined with one-to-one optimal-transport constraints reduced pseudo-label errors — [UPL-EA, DMKD 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-025-01128-0)

### Inferences
- **Conditional independence is badly violated between (b) and (c).** Both are rerankers trained on the same US/India pairs. The GBDT uses different inputs (string features), so its errors are likely more independent. With only three sources, Dawid–Skene or spectral estimates will overweight the CE–Qwen consensus. **Recommended combination (ranked):**
  1. Calibrate each model to France using the probe anchors (Q3), then average probabilities, possibly weighting the GBDT more. Or use a rank average when calibration is doubtful.
  2. Use a **disagreement-targeted rule**. The CE false negatives come from legal-form or abbreviation artefacts, so let a high-confidence GBDT positive *rescue* a CE negative when the name core and address agree. Keep CE and Qwen as vetoes only where they are confident *and* the GBDT is uncertain. This protects the ≈0.97 precision while recovering recall from the 0.82–0.92 range.
  3. Majority vote over thresholded outputs, as a robust fallback that is hard to overfit.
  4. SML, Dawid–Skene or Platanios accuracy estimates as a **diagnostic**: compare implied per-model balanced accuracy on France with the same computation on US/India TUNE. A model whose France/TUNE ratio collapses is the one misfiring.
- A **ZeroER-style two-component GMM** on the GBDT's string-feature vectors, or on the 3-model score vector, restricted to France, gives an independent estimate of the French link prior and of posterior match probability. Use it as a sanity check against the calibrated-GBDT estimate, not as a production scorer.
- Agreement-on-the-line needs many model pairs to fit a line. Three models give three pairs, so treat it as a qualitative check at most. It also does not apply to GBDT pairs.
- Any combiner chosen by looking at French score distributions is still selected on France without labels. It should be frozen before any public submission is compared, so the leaderboard subset is not used to tune (per project guardrails).

### Gaps
- No entity-matching-specific benchmark was found that compares MV against Dawid–Skene/Snorkel against rank averaging for combining **model** outputs (as opposed to labelling functions) under language shift.
- I did not retrieve ZeroER's per-dataset F1 table, only the abstract-level claim of "comparable to supervised".

## Q3. How can thresholds and models be selected without labels (known truth rates on subsets as calibration anchors, class-prior estimation, matching predicted positive rates to expected link counts, mixture modelling of score distributions)?

### Takeaway
This is the highest-value, lowest-compute area. Three mature families apply: (1) prior-shift adaptation of calibrated probabilities (Saerens EM/SLD, which a 2021 review found hard to beat); (2) quantification by adjusted counts with robust threshold policies (Median Sweep, TSMax) or mixture/distribution matching (Forman MM, HDy/DyS); (3) confidence-based accuracy estimation (ATC). The two probe subsets with known truth rates are a rare asset. They act as bag-level labels, as in learning from label proportions, and two bags are exactly enough to fit a two-parameter Platt recalibration of each model on France.

### Cited Findings
- Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker (Neural Computation 2002) give an iterative EM procedure to adjust a trained classifier's posteriors to new, unknown priors "without having to refit the model", and it yields estimates of the new priors as a by-product — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Adjusting-the-Outputs-of-a-Classifier-to-New-a-A-Saerens-Latinne/99f65a048860d3f2bc500a2886c461d92188d2ca); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1162/089976602753284446)
- The prior-shift review (Sipka et al., "Hitchhiker's Guide") reports that "adapting a calibrated classifier to new priors estimated by EM outperforms the re-trained classifiers using BBSL and RLLS, making EM with Bias-Corrected Calibration 'Hard-To-Beat'". It also notes that EM is equivalent to minimising KL between the new input density and the prior-reweighted mixture — [arXiv 2106.11695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11695)
- Esuli, Molinari & Sebastiani published "A critical reassessment of the SLD algorithm" (ACM TOIS), which re-examines EM prior and posterior adjustment under distribution shift — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3433164) (I did not retrieve its conclusions; see Gaps)
- Quantification (Forman): "Classify and Count" is biased. It underestimates prevalence above a certain point and overestimates it below. Adjusted Count corrects it as p = (p_CC − fpr)/(tpr − fpr), with tpr and fpr estimated by cross-validation on training data. Threshold policies improve stability: Max (maximise tpr − fpr), X (fpr = 1 − tpr), T50 (tpr = 0.5), and Median Sweep (median over all thresholds with tpr − fpr ≥ 0.25) — [Estimating minority class proportion (thesis summarising Forman 2008)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/qn5s3lgjb8x); [Unified Framework for Quantification, arXiv 1606.00868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00868v1.pdf)
- Forman's Median Sweep "show[s] outstanding ability to estimate the class distribution, despite wide disparity in testing and training conditions" — [Forman, HP Labs HPL-2006-48R1](http://shiftleft.com/mirrors/www.hpl.hp.com/techreports/2006/HPL-2006-48R1.html)
- A comparison of 24 quantification methods on more than 40 datasets (JMLR 2025) found no single winner. In the binary setting the best group was **Median Sweep, TSMax, DyS/HDy, Forman's mixture model and Friedman's method**. Methods that optimise a classifier specifically for quantification did worse on average, and "tuning the underlying classifiers has in most cases only a limited impact" — [Schumacher et al., JMLR v26](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v26/21-0241.html); [arXiv 2103.03223](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03223v5)
- Forman's mixture model matches the cumulative distributions of classifier scores (per-class training CDFs mixed by unknown proportions) to the test score CDF, minimising the PP-area (L1). HDy does the same with Hellinger distance on binned PDFs — [arXiv 1606.00868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00868v1.pdf); [arXiv 2103.03223](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03223v5)
- Class-prior estimation from an unlabelled mixture p(x) = π p(x|y=1) + (1 − π) p(x|y=−1) can be done by fitting class-wise densities with penalised divergences — [du Plessis/Sugiyama et al., Machine Learning 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-016-5604-6)
- ATC (Garg et al., ICLR 2022) learns a confidence threshold on source validation data so that the fraction of examples above it equals source accuracy. It predicts target accuracy as the fraction of unlabelled target examples above that threshold, and estimates target performance "2–4× more accurately than prior methods". The authors prove that, in general, "identifying the accuracy is just as hard as identifying the optimal predictor", so any method relies on assumptions about the shift — [arXiv 2201.04234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234); [ICLR poster](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6847)
- Models calibrated on source data "are not, in general, calibrated on a (previously unseen) target domain" (citing Ovadia et al.) — [Garg et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234)
- Learning from Label Proportions is the weak-supervision setting where "examples are grouped into 'bags' and only the average label within each bag is revealed" — [Optimistic Rates for LLP, arXiv 2406.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00487)
- ZeroER-style GMMs on similarity vectors give unsupervised match and non-match densities, and therefore a label-free posterior and prior — [ZeroER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06049)

### Inferences
- **Use the probe subsets as two LLP bags to recalibrate (b) and (c) on France.** Platt recalibration p' = σ(a·logit(s) + b) has two parameters, and two bags with known mean truth rates (≈0.665 and ≈0.94, taking interval midpoints) pin down (a, b). Do this for the Frenchified CE and Qwen. Then check the fit on anything held out, for example a third French subset whose truth rate is inferable, or the GBDT's per-bin agreement. Caveats: (i) two equations and two unknowns means zero residual degrees of freedom, so fit error cannot be detected; (ii) the probe truth rates are intervals (64–69%, 92–96%), so propagate the endpoints to get a threshold band; (iii) the probes may not be representative of the full French candidate pool. That last one is the main risk.
- The **GBDT already reproduces both probe rates.** Treat its French probabilities as calibrated. (1) Sum its probabilities over all French top-candidate pairs to get an **expected number of true French links**. (2) Run Saerens EM, starting from those probabilities, to refine the French prior. (3) Cross-check with Median Sweep or Forman MM, using US/India TUNE to estimate per-threshold tpr and fpr for the CE (this assumes p(score|y) transfers, which is exactly what fails for the misfiring CE; so run it for the GBDT and the Frenchified CE only). If the three prevalence estimates disagree by more than a few points, the recalibrated transformer scores should not be trusted.
- **Threshold rule for F0.5 with calibrated probabilities.** Sort French pairs by the combined calibrated probability. For each cut-off k, compute expected TP = Σ top-k p, expected FP = k − TP, and expected FN = (total expected links) − TP. Pick k to maximise expected F0.5 = 1.25·TP / (1.25·TP + 0.25·FN + FP). This uses only unlabelled scores and the calibrated prior. Because the objective is macro F0.5, run it per S1 or group, following the official singleton convention, rather than globally. A related known result, from Lipton et al. 2014 on thresholding to maximise F1 (not fetched this session, verify before citing): with calibrated scores, the F1-optimal threshold equals half the optimal F1. The F-beta analogue implies a threshold near F*/(1+β²) → ≈0.8·F* for β = 0.5, which is higher than for F1. Treat this as a cross-check, not a source.
- **Matching predicted positive counts to expected link counts** is Classify-and-Count in reverse. It is only sound if the prevalence estimate comes from a bias-corrected method (EM, Adjusted Count or mixture model). The raw count of above-0.5 predictions from a misfiring CE is biased (Forman). Target a slightly *lower* count than the expected-link estimate, since F0.5 penalises false positives more.
- **Model selection without labels, in order:** (1) calibration error on the two probe bags before recalibration (large misfit signals shift); (2) agreement with the calibrated GBDT on French pairs, stratified by GBDT confidence; (3) ATC-estimated French accuracy, with the ATC threshold learned on US/India TUNE; (4) the prevalence implied by each model after Saerens EM. A model is preferred only if it is consistent on all four. None of these is proof, since Garg et al. show accuracy estimation needs assumptions about the shift.
- Keep a **precision-leaning near-tie**, as project guidance says. With French precision already ≈0.97, recall is the main lever. Accept a lower threshold only where the calibrated-GBDT expected-FP increase is below the expected-TP gain × 0.25, which is the F0.5 break-even.

## Q4. How do LLM-based matchers transfer across languages or domains compared with fine-tuned BERT-class models (Peeters et al.; Steiner et al. 2024)?

### Takeaway
The Mannheim group's evidence is consistent. Fine-tuned PLM matchers (RoBERTa, Ditto) lose 22–61 F1 points when moved to unseen entities or datasets. Zero-shot LLMs degrade far less. Fine-tuning an LLM helps in-domain but tends to hurt cross-domain transfer. For language transfer specifically, mixing source-language pairs into training always helped a German matcher, and multilingual PLMs work for non-English product matching. No French business-name benchmark exists, so all of this is indirect evidence. The team's own finding that the original CE misfires on French legal forms fits the "fine-tuned PLMs are not robust out of distribution" result.

### Cited Findings
- Peeters, Steiner & Bizer (EDBT 2025, arXiv 2310.11244): fine-tuned PLMs "require significant amounts of task-specific training data" and "are not robust concerning out-of-distribution entities". The best LLMs "require no or only a few training examples to perform comparably to PLMs that were fine-tuned using thousands of examples" and "exhibit higher robustness to unseen entities" — [arXiv 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)
- In that paper, fine-tuned RoBERTa and Ditto transferred to the WDC Products test set ("unseen") dropped 22–61 F1 for RoBERTa and 36–56 F1 for Ditto, compared with Ditto trained directly on WDC Products (84.90 F1). Transferred RoBERTa scored 16.25–55.52 F1, and transferred Ditto 29.00–48.74. "All LLMs achieve at least 8% F1 higher performance than the best transferred PLM while GPT4 outperforms the best PLM by 40% to 68%" — [arXiv 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)
- The same paper found "no single best prompt": prompts must be tuned per model and dataset. Zero-shot GPT-4 beat fine-tuned PLMs on 3 of 4 e-commerce datasets. Fine-tuning improved LLMs by 1–26 F1 over their best zero-shot, but "fine-tuning reduces generalizability for the Llama models" while GPT-mini kept cross-dataset generalisation — [arXiv 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)
- Transfer direction matters. GPT-mini fine-tuned on product data generalised to the publication domain (+1–3 F1 over best zero-shot), but the reverse transfer lowered performance on product datasets — [arXiv 2310.11244](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)
- Steiner, Peeters & Bizer (ICDE 2025, arXiv 2409.08185): "fine-tuning significantly improves the performance of the smaller models while the results for the larger models are mixed. Fine-tuning also improves the generalization to in-domain datasets while hurting cross-domain transfer". Fine-tuned models' cross-domain performance "falls short of the zero-shot baseline in the majority of cases". Adding structured explanations to training examples helped 3 of 4 LLMs. LLM-based example selection and generation helped Llama 3.1 8B but hurt GPT-4o-mini — [arXiv 2409.08185](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08185)
- Cross-language learning for product matching (Peeters & Bizer, WWW 2022 Companion): "extending the German set with English pairs improves the matching performance in all cases". The impact is "especially high in low-resource settings", and combining English pairs with a small German set exceeded 90% F1. Models tested: BERT, German BERT, mBERT, XLM-R and an SVM. Note: this setup still used some German labels — [arXiv 2110.03338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.03338)
- A University of Mannheim student project built a pair-wise cross-lingual product test set (more than 4,000 verified pairs in English, German, Spanish and **French**) and found Transformer models "leverage their contextual and multilingual sensitivity on the pair-wise" task — [Cross-lingual Product Matching using Transformers](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/Web-based-Systems-Group/StudentProjects/2020/Cross-lingual-Product-Matching-using-Transformers/)
- Billiger.de Products (Steiner et al., Mannheim/solute) is a bilingual German/machine-translated-English entity matching benchmark: 13,730 offers, 2,168 products, Seen/Half-Seen/Unseen test sets. It is validated with six supervised matchers and zero-shot GPT-5.2, and includes a cross-language experiment where German-trained matchers are evaluated on mixed German–English pairs — [Billiger.de Products](https://wbsg-uni-mannheim.github.io/billiger-de-products/)
- Fine-tuned multilingual mBERT and XLM-R models work for product matching in English (WDC) and Polish (new ProductMatch.pl dataset) — [Mozdzonek et al., arXiv 2205.15712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15712v2.pdf)
- Cross-lingual learning using English data to train Portuguese product matchers "achieve[s] promising results ... using a reduced volume of training data in the target language" and beats baselines across several cross-lingual learning strategies and LLMs — [ACM SAC 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3605098.3636001)
- LLM-based matchers "are less susceptible to the unseen entity problem" than deep-learning matchers. A COLING 2025 paper argues that binary pairwise matching ignores global consistency, and proposes "selecting" among candidates (listwise) after a pairwise pre-filter — [COLING 2025, "Match, Compare, or Select?"](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.8.pdf)
- In instruction tuning, English-only tuning transfers cross-lingually "only if multiliguality is taken into account in hyperparameter tuning and with large enough IT data" — [Chirkova & Nikoulina, INLG 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.inlg-main.53)

### Inferences
- A **Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B fine-tuned on US/India** falls in the "small model, fine-tuned" category, where Steiner et al. found in-domain gains but hurt cross-domain transfer. Its France behaviour may be closer to the fine-tuned-PLM pattern than to zero-shot-LLM robustness. Two checks are worth running: compare the **un-fine-tuned or lightly-tuned Qwen reranker** on the French probe bags against the fine-tuned version (calibration error before recalibration), and check whether fine-tuning damaged its handling of legal forms.
- The literature supports Frenchify as the right lever. It is the zero-label analogue of "adding target-language-like pairs", which helped in every Peeters & Bizer configuration. Rule-generated perturbations (legal forms, street-type abbreviations, accents, decoy words), applied to *training* records only, match the rules constraint. Extend it with French legal-form **equivalence as positives** (X ≡ X SARL ≡ X S.A.R.L.) and **legal-form-differs-but-otherwise-identical** pairs, labelled by the US/India convention for the same case.
- The GBDT should be kept in any French ensemble. It is language-agnostic and already calibrated on France, and every cited transfer study shows fine-tuned transformers are the component most likely to fail under shift.
- A listwise "select among candidates" formulation, from the COLING 2025 paper and the repo's existing `listwise_ce.py`, adds a one-to-one or global-consistency prior. Per the entity-alignment self-training results, this should also reduce false positives from pairwise misfires.
