# Last-mile techniques in entity-resolution / record-matching competitions (post-processing, assignment, clustering, ensembling, thresholds)

Scope note: the target task is a per-reference-entity macro F0.5 with a one-owner constraint (each target record belongs to at most one reference entity), at most 11 matches per reference, and singletons scoring 1 only when the prediction is empty. The closest competition analogues are Foursquare Location Matching 2022 (per-id IoU averaged over ids), Shopee Price Match 2021 (per-row F1 averaged over rows), and Quora Question Pairs 2017 (pairwise logloss, so less directly relevant). None of these had a one-owner constraint. Shopee guaranteed at least 2 items per group, which is the *opposite* of a singleton-empty convention. Tricks that exploit a guaranteed minimum size (for example "min2") therefore transfer only in inverted form.

### Ranked summary (for the report writer; ranks are my judgement, based on the gains cited below)
1. **Extra ensemble diversity (models from different teams or families: GBDT + cross-encoders), with the threshold tuned jointly.** Reported +0.010 (Shopee 2nd, team merge), +0.004 CV (Foursquare 1st, 4-model blend over the best single model), and +0.003 to +0.005 LB (Foursquare 9th). This is the most consistently reported gain.
2. **More training entities/pairs for the second-stage GBDT/matcher.** Foursquare 8th reports LB 0.908 → 0.928 → 0.948 as training ids grew from 550k to 700k to 1.1M. Foursquare 9th reports +0.010 LB from training on all data.
3. **Set-level and graph-level post-processing that uses the neighbours' predictions.** Reported gains: +0.0056 CV (Foursquare 1st: merge two ids' match lists at more than 50% overlap, then re-score the new pairs), +0.005 LB (Foursquare 9th: average a node's scores with its neighbours'), about +0.009 (Shopee 3rd: agglomerative clustering to a target mean cluster size), and +0.001 (Shopee 2nd: removing bridge edges by betweenness). Foursquare's blog describes a GNN node-classification post-process as the step that took one competitor from 0.907 to 0.946. The attribution is unclear (see Gaps).
4. **Constraints and priors on list size.** Shopee's min2 gave +0.019 on the 1st-place ladder and +0.007 test F1 in an independent replication. The analogue for the singleton-empty convention is an explicit "empty vs. non-empty" decision, for example expected-F maximisation (Ye et al. 2012), as used in Instacart.
5. **One-to-one / one-owner global assignment instead of independent thresholds.** Large gains over *unconstrained* many-to-many resolution: knee precision/recall of about 90/90 vs 81/69 (MSR). Gains over *greedy* one-to-one are small or negative. Greedy unique-mapping (UMC) and mutual-best are usually on par with or better than exact max-weight (Hungarian). Hungarian/Sinkhorn win mainly on dense embedding-similarity matrices.
6. **Listwise / record-centric selection vs pairwise.** Large gains for zero-shot LLMs: +16 to +17.6% avg F1, and ComEM adds up to 4% more. I found no competition-grade evidence that a fine-tuned listwise model beats a strong fine-tuned pairwise cross-encoder + GBDT stack.
7. **Multi-route blocking with a learned pruner.** This is a precondition for everything else. Top Foursquare teams used 2 or more routes, got a max-IoU ceiling of 0.979 to 0.994, then pruned with a cheap GBDT at threshold 0.005 to 0.01, losing only about 0.005 to 0.007 of the ceiling. A union of DL and rule-based blocking adds +0.2 to +14.5 absolute recall (DeepBlocker).

## Q1. Which post-processing / ensembling / threshold steps in top competition solutions gave measurable gains, and how much?

### Takeaway
In the three best-documented matching competitions, the last-mile gains stacked roughly as follows. Ensembling heterogeneous matchers gave +0.004 to +0.010. Graph or set-level post-processing gave +0.001 to +0.009 (up to +0.019 for set-size priors that exploit a known group-size rule). Tuning a few final thresholds jointly with the post-processing also mattered. Scaling training data gave the largest single non-leak gain reported (+0.04 LB in Foursquare 8th). In Foursquare, and to a lesser degree Quora, leaks dwarfed all modelling gains, so leaderboard deltas there need care.

### Cited Findings

**Foursquare Location Matching 2022 (metric: mean per-id IoU)**
- 1st place (re:waiwai) used a 4-stage pipeline. Stage 1: 100 candidates each from lat/lon Euclidean kNN and from multilingual-BERT name-embedding kNN; a LightGBM kept the top 20 per route (about 40 per id); max IoU 0.979. Stage 2: a LightGBM with about 120 features at threshold 0.01 removed about 90% of the candidates at CV 0.875; max IoU after stage 2 was 0.974. Stage 3: CatBoost, xlm-roberta-large and mdeberta-v3-base (mdeberta CV 0.907). Ensemble weights: 0.01 × stage-2 LGB + 0.32 × CatBoost + 0.29 × xlm-r-large + 0.38 × mdeberta, giving **CV 0.911** at threshold 0.5. Stage 4 post-processing: "Compare the matches of two ids and merge the matches of two ids if the common id exceeds 50% from either side", then re-predict the newly created pairs with xlm-roberta-large at threshold 0.02, giving **CV 0.9166** (+0.0056 over the ensemble) — [1st place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution); [code repo](https://github.com/TakoiHirokazu/Kaggle-Foursquare-Location-Matching)
- 1st place leak: tying test to train by name/lat/lon moved stage-2 LB from 0.900 to 0.943 (add train-train true positives), then 0.957, then 0.971 (also remove false positives). Final private scores were 0.941 without the leak merge and 0.977 with it. The leak dominated the leaderboard — [1st place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution); [Chinese summary with the (1)+(2) = 0.957 step](https://jishu.proginn.com/doc/7780647801f904d12)
- 2nd place: final LB 0.949 without the leak and 0.971 with it. The stack was an ensemble of XGBoost, LightGBM and xlm-roberta-base pairwise classifiers. The GBDTs used many RapidFuzz edit-distance, Jaccard/Dice and TF-IDF (word and char 1–3-gram) cosine features, plus the transformer probability as a feature. The team deliberately grouped CV folds by src_id instead of POI to exploit train/test record overlap — [2nd place colum2131 part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/2-30-2nd-place-solution-colum2131-part)
- 2nd place blocking: 128 candidates per id from lat/lon nearest neighbours and name-TF-IDF cosine, mixed with **country-specific optimized ratios**, gave max IoU 0.9895. A transformer "blocker" (single LB 0.918) at threshold 0.005, followed by a **union-find post-process**, left an average of 4.1 candidates per id at max IoU 0.986 — [2nd place T0m part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072)
- 9th place LB ladder: CatBoost 0.925 → +post-process 0.930 → +LightGBM 0.933 → +training on all data 0.943 → +5-model ensemble 0.945 → +more iterations 0.948. The post-process was "calc the node predictions with avg of neighbors predictions, order by predictions", threshold 0.4 — [9th place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336415)
- 8th place: public LB rose from 0.908 (550k training ids) to 0.928 (700k) to 0.948 (1.1M ids; 25M pairs; 7 k-fold LightGBMs). Post-processing was unchanged from the public baseline — [8th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/kita-kyushu-lovers-8th-place-solution-sbert-lightg)
- 7th place post-process: keep edges above a threshold; remove some edges by **betweenness centrality**; form groups with union-find; predict, for each vertex, the vertices within unweighted graph distance 2, capped at 256. No delta was reported — [7th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/in-tokyo-7th-place-solution-with-inference-code)
- Foursquare's official recap says one top competitor (Yuki Uehara, a 2nd-place team member) used "post processing node classification by a Graph Neural Network" and "was able to improve his score from 0.907 to 0.946". The blog does not say whether the whole gain came from the GNN — [Foursquare blog](https://foursquare.com/resources/blog/developer/finding-the-right-poi-match/)

**Shopee Price Match Guarantee 2021 (metric: mean per-row F1; every item has at least one other match)**
- 1st place ablation ladder: image-only 0.70 / text-only 0.64 → concat embeddings 0.724 → **min2 0.743 (+0.019)** → normalize before concat 0.753 → full-data training 0.757 → **union of comb/img/txt matches + threshold tuning 0.776 (+0.019)** → **INB (Iterative Neighborhood Blending, akin to query expansion / DBA) + diverse text models 0.784** → INB with img/txt/comb at stage 1 + joint threshold tuning **0.793** — [1st place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/upstage-making-ai-beneficial-1st-place-solution-fr)
- 1st place definition of min2: at least 2 matches per query are guaranteed by the task, so the second-closest match is rejected only if its distance exceeds a high min2 threshold (~0.8). The team had 10 thresholds, but "major score boost came from stage2 and stage3 threshold", so in the end they tuned only 2 numbers — [1st place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/upstage-making-ai-beneficial-1st-place-solution-fr)
- Independent replication of the 1st-place ideas on a held-out split: test F1 0.8211 → min2 0.8285 (+0.0074) → INB 0.8345 (+0.006) — [jingxuanyang blog](http://www.jingxuanyang.com/2021/05/30/Shopee-Product-Matching/)
- 2nd place: merging two teammates' pipelines (0.767 and 0.765) gave 0.777, "huge improvement… not sure why". Further ladder steps: more text models +0.003 each, +pagerank feature +0.001, +query expansion +0.001, +GAT ensembling +0.002, final 0.792. Their ensembling rule subtracts each model's own best threshold, sums the margins, predicts a match if the sum is above 0, and then **drops pairs without a mutual edge** (A–B kept only if B–A also kept). Graph post-processing that recursively removes the highest-betweenness edge between cliques "pushed our score by about 0.001". Sample weighting was 1/(group size)^0.4 — [2nd place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/lyaka-tkm-2nd-place-solution-matching-prediction-b)
- 3rd place: candidates came from the union of radius-neighbours over many embeddings (EffNet, Indonesian BERT, LaBSE, several TF-IDFs, CLIP), then a CatBoost with about 500 features (distances, density around both points, **ranks**). This gave about 0.76 LB, and 0.781 with fine-tuned models. Agglomerative clustering on GBM probabilities, merging until the **average cluster size reached a target** (2.8 on train, 2.6 best on LB), with forced attachment of singletons, "boost me to 0.79" (about +0.009) — [3rd place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/btbpanda-3rd-place-solution-triplet-loss-boosting-)

**Quora Question Pairs 2017 (metric: logloss; graph "magic" = leak-like structure)**
- 3rd place: best single XGB CV about 0.185; stacking about 15 models with meta-features (question frequency, intersection counts, per-question mean/min of predictions) reached CV 0.157. A final blend of 0.75 stacked + 0.25 base models gave about +0.001 public LB (it hurt CV but transferred to private). They also rescaled predictions by question frequency (odds reduced by a factor of 4.7 when both questions were unique), because only ~3% of unique-question pairs in the public LB were duplicates vs ~25% in train — [3rd place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/quora-question-pairs/writeups/jared-turkewitz-sjv-overview-of-3rd-place-solution)
- 3rd place graph features: a few hundred graph features (intersection counts, neighbourhood overlap, PageRank, degree, shortest path, clique sizes), also computed on a graph weighted by out-of-fold predictions — [3rd place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/quora-question-pairs/writeups/jared-turkewitz-sjv-overview-of-3rd-place-solution)
- 4th place: best single XGB private 0.13067 (no post-processing) vs final stack private 0.11768 with post-processing. The post-processing cut the data by **clique size** and rescaled each part separately. The write-up does not separate the stacking gain from the post-processing gain — [HouJP repo](https://github.com/houjp/kaggle-quora-question-pairs/)
- 2nd place: a weighted ensemble of 6 LightGBM + 1 NN, then recalibration "based on some graphical properties". The best single superset LGB was 0.116–0.117 LB — [2nd place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/quora-question-pairs/writeups/depp-learning-overview-of-2nd-place-solution)
- The max k-core feature was reported as +0.003 to +0.0055 LB by several forum users — [Kaggle discussion 33371](https://www.kaggle.com/c/quora-question-pairs/discussion/33371)
- The test positive rate was about 16–17% vs about 34% in train, and most teams rescaled for it. This is a secondary source — [Medium write-up](https://medium.com/data-science/identifying-duplicate-questions-on-quora-top-12-on-kaggle-4c1cf93f1c30)

**SIGMOD Programming Contest (ER editions 2020–2022)**
- 2020 (camera specs) and 2021 (USB sticks/notebooks) were scored on (macro) F-measure. In 2020 "all top-5 teams reached a 0.99 F-measure", and running time broke the tie. Winners relied on heavy preprocessing, rule/regex extraction of brand and model, blocking by brand/model, and mostly rule-based matching. In 2021 some teams combined XGBoost or random forests with rule-based matchers — [organisers' lessons-learned report (De Angelis et al., 2023)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/9r6kybbr8rk)
- 2022 was a blocking-only edition (recall at a fixed 1M/2M candidate budget). The winner, WBSG (Mannheim), used a tiny distilled transformer trained with supervised contrastive learning plus extra Common Crawl product offers, FAISS kNN, and **re-ranking by the average of cosine and Jaccard similarity** to "smooth the bias of the embedding model" — [WBSG repo](https://github.com/abrinkmann/acm_sigmoid_2022_challenge); [Uni Mannheim news](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/en/news/wbsg-wins-sigmod-programming-contest-2022/)
- The WBSG poster lists average recall 0.529 (D1 0.713, D2 0.345). A runner-up poster based on similarity joins lists 0.520 (D1 0.743, D2 0.297). It is unclear whether these are final leaderboard numbers — [WBSG poster](https://dbgroup.ing.unimo.it/sigmod22contest/posters/WBSG.pdf); [April poster](https://dbgroup.ing.unimo.it/sigmod22contest/posters/April.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across Foursquare and Shopee, **set-level/graph post-processing is worth about +0.001 to +0.009** on top of a strong matcher. The larger gains (+0.0056 CV, +0.005 LB, ~+0.009) came from steps that *use neighbours' predictions to add or remove whole groups*: overlap-based merging with re-scoring, neighbour-averaged node scores, and clustering to a size prior. Pure edge pruning (betweenness) gave only about +0.001.
- The Foursquare 1st-place merge step re-scores the *newly proposed* pairs with the strongest model instead of accepting them blindly. The same pattern applies to one-owner ER: any post-process that proposes new links or reassignments should be verified by the matcher.
- Shopee's min2 exploited a known minimum group size. For a task where singletons score 1 only if empty, the analogous lever is the opposite: a calibrated "predict empty" decision per reference. In Instacart, teams handled the "None" case with expected-F1 maximisation (Ye et al. 2012 / Faron optimizer), selecting per-query how many top candidates to output — [panyao Instacart repo](https://github.com/panyao/instacart-solution); [alexanderrich Instacart repo](https://github.com/alexanderrich/instacart-analysis). I found no concrete gain figure for this (see Gaps).
- The Quora "odds adjustment by frequency" and the Shopee "target average cluster size" are both **segment-wise calibration to a shifted prior**. With known country or script shift, per-segment thresholds or odds scaling may matter more than model changes. This is an inference, and it carries a real overfitting risk on a reused tune set.
- Leaderboard ladders from leaky competitions (Foursquare, Quora) overstate the transferable value of some steps. The Foursquare CV-based numbers (1st place) and the Shopee ablations are the cleanest.

### Gaps
- The Foursquare 3rd-, 4th-, 5th- and 13th-place (GNN) write-ups did not render (Kaggle blocked them). The 4th-place repo has a `pp.py`, but its gain was not retrieved.
- The 2nd-place GNN post-process: the 0.907 → 0.946 figure comes from a Foursquare marketing blog and may include other stages. No ablation was found.
- No SIGMOD 2020/2021 winning write-up with per-step F-measure deltas was found. The organisers' report only describes approaches qualitatively.
- No Amazon-hosted product-matching challenge with published last-mile ablations was found in this pass.

## Q2. With a one-owner constraint, when does global assignment (Hungarian / min-cost flow) beat greedy best-score resolution, and by how much?

### Takeaway
Imposing the one-to-one or one-owner constraint at all yields the big win over independent thresholding (roughly +8 to +20 points of precision/recall at the knee in the MSR movie study). *Among* constrained methods, the differences are small. Greedy best-first (UMC, Greedy) and mutual-best are usually as good as or better than exact max-weight matching on sparse, well-calibrated classifier scores. Exact assignment (Hungarian) or Sinkhorn wins mainly on dense embedding-similarity matrices where many-to-one collisions are common. Maximising total score is not the same as maximising F-measure.

### Cited Findings
- MSR (Gupta, Rubinstein et al.) compared ManyMany (threshold only), FirstChoice (one-to-many), and three one-to-one methods: MutualFirstChoice, Greedy (sort by score, accept if both sides are free) and MaxWeight (Hungarian). One-to-one methods reached precision and recall of about 90% at the knee, vs about 83%/81% for FirstChoice and 81%/69% for ManyMany. Differences among the one-to-one algorithms were "statistically insignificant". "Maximizing matching weight does not necessarily optimize performance of one-to-one ER." Greedy agreed with 98% of 13,005 Freebase links and was 99% precise on a sample of extra links — [MSR one-to-one ER report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/msr-report-1to1.pdf)
- MSR example: MaxWeight "can be misled by a number of very similar" candidates (a "Making of" or "Bonus Material" variant), adopting diagonal arcs, while Greedy matched both correctly — [MSR report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/msr-report-1to1.pdf)
- Papadakis et al. (VLDB Journal 2023) evaluated 8 bipartite matching algorithms over hundreds of similarity graphs. **UMC (Unique Mapping Clustering, a greedy best-first 1-1 method)** was the best choice for balanced datasets (#Top1 on 85 of 196 graphs), and outperformed Király's stable-marriage approximation (KRC) by about 1.5% F1 on average. **KRC** led on one-sided and scarce datasets (e.g. #Top1 166 of 420 scarce graphs, with EXC, the mutual-best method, close behind at 147). BAH, a max-weight assignment heuristic, is "capable of the best and the worst" and is about 15× slower than UMC. EXC and UMC offered "the best balance between F1 and run-time". UMC beat the unsupervised ZeroER by 3–83% F1 — [An analysis of one-to-one matching algorithms for ER](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00778-023-00791-3)
- In a KG entity-alignment benchmark of matching algorithms: on PARIS (sparse, near-bipolar scores), stable matching improved F1 by about 1.9/1.3/1.4% on three datasets, while *all assignment algorithms (including Hungarian) lowered F1 below native PARIS*. On dense embedding similarities (GCN-Align, RREA), Hungarian and Sinkhorn were best, and stable matching was 1–7% lower than Hungarian. Greedy argmax (DInf, equivalent to Hits@1) was the weakest — [Benchmarking matching algorithms for EA (arXiv 2308.03961)](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03961)
- SEU (EMNLP 2021) cast EA as an assignment problem. Hungarian was "always slightly better" than Sinkhorn, but the gap was small, and Sinkhorn is O(kn²), GPU-parallel and more stable in time — [SEU paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.226.pdf)
- CEA (collective EA via deferred acceptance / stable matching): removing the collective step "drops performance on all datasets" — [CEA paper (arXiv 1912.08404)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08404)
- OpenEA benchmark: all methods then used greedy search. Collective alternatives include Kuhn-Munkres O(N³), a heuristic linear-time approximation, and stable marriage — [Sun et al., VLDB 2020](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p2326-sun.pdf)
- Competition practice: Shopee 2nd place used a *mutual-edge* filter (drop A–B unless B–A also survives) — [Shopee 2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/lyaka-tkm-2nd-place-solution-matching-prediction-b). Foursquare 1st/2nd/7th used union-find or overlap merging, which are transitive-closure style rather than assignment — [Foursquare 2nd T0m part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072); [7th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/in-tokyo-7th-place-solution-with-inference-code)

### Inferences
- Our task is one-owner on the target side only (a target record belongs to at most one reference; a reference may take up to 11 targets). That makes it a capacitated b-matching problem: reference capacity ≤ 11, target capacity ≤ 1. It is solvable exactly by min-cost flow. The greedy analogue sorts all (reference, target) edges above threshold by calibrated probability and assigns each target to the first reference with capacity. The evidence above suggests exact min-cost flow will rarely beat this greedy by more than a few tenths of a point, and it can lose when scores are not calibrated probabilities. Maximising summed probability is not F0.5-optimal. This is an estimate extrapolated from the one-to-one literature, not measured on b-matching.
- The measurable win should come from **resolving target conflicts at all**, i.e. removing the lower-scored duplicate claims, compared with independent per-pair thresholds. That win is largest when many targets receive above-threshold scores from two or more references (dense near-duplicate references, chains, franchises). Counting conflicted targets on the tune set gives an upper bound on the gain before any solver is built.
- For F0.5 (precision-weighted), the MSR and Papadakis results favour conservative constrained rules (mutual-best / EXC, UMC with a threshold) over maximum-weight rules, which fill capacity with marginal edges.

### Gaps
- No competition write-up found reports a direct Hungarian/min-cost-flow vs greedy A/B for a one-to-many, capacity-constrained setting.
- The Papadakis study uses unsupervised similarity scores (syntactic/semantic), not supervised classifier probabilities. Its ranking may differ with a calibrated cross-encoder.

## Q3. Evidence on listwise / record-centric models (models that see all competing candidates) vs pairwise models

### Takeaway
For prompted LLMs, letting the model see the competing candidates (compare or select) gives large average F1 gains over independent pairwise matching: +10.7% (compare) and +16 to 17.6% (select). A cheap pairwise pre-filter plus listwise selection over the top-k (ComEM) adds up to 4% more at lower cost. Listwise models suffer strong position bias, and the best k is dataset-dependent (k = 1 was best on an easy dataset). Competition winners got "record-centric" information into the model indirectly: GBDT features over the candidate set (ranks, neighbourhood density, top-K similarity statistics, graph features) and GNN/GAT over the candidate graph, not end-to-end listwise transformers.

### Cited Findings
- ComEM (COLING 2025): the selecting strategy improved F1 by an average of 16.02% over the matching strategy (v3; v2 reported 17.58%). Comparing beat matching by 10.7% F1 on average, and select beat compare by 5.32%. Selecting still beat 6-shot matching by 7.97% F1 (same number of records in context). ComEM (pairwise filter, then select among the top k) improved further by up to 4% while spending less: for GPT-3.5, 85.61% F1 at $0.92 vs 81.60% F1 at $1.71 for select-only. Filtering "improves precision considerably (6.7%)" — [arXiv 2405.16884v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3); [v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v2); [alphaXiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2405.16884)
- ComEM caveats: "selection accuracy decreases greatly as the position of the matching record increases in the candidate list". The best k varies (k = 1 best on DBLP-ACM; F1 swings sharply between k = 2 and 5 elsewhere). Experiments were zero/few-shot, and fine-tuning was left as future work — [arXiv 2405.16884v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v2)
- Selective EM with hard negatives (Mistral4SelectEM, 2025): benchmarks were recompiled so each query has 10 embedding-retrieved candidates. SOTA pairwise matchers degrade under these hard-negative conditions. A listwise Siamese LLM fine-tuned with a contrastive margin ranking loss "outperforms SOTA pairwise EM approaches in both efficiency and performance". I did not retrieve exact numbers — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474034625004318); [code and data](https://github.com/quickhdsdc/LLM4EntityMatching)
- Competition analogues: Shopee 3rd place gave its GBM features computed *relative to each item's neighbourhood* ("density around both points", "points ranks") across many embeddings — [Shopee 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/btbpanda-3rd-place-solution-triplet-loss-boosting-). Shopee 2nd added avg/std of top-K similarities (K = 5, 10, 15, 30) normalised per item, PageRank, and a GAT over "other edges connected to the node connected to the target edge", worth +0.001 (PageRank) and +0.002 (GAT) — [Shopee 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/lyaka-tkm-2nd-place-solution-matching-prediction-b). Quora 3rd stacked with per-question mean/min of first-level predictions — [Quora 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/quora-question-pairs/writeups/jared-turkewitz-sjv-overview-of-3rd-place-solution)
- Foursquare 2nd place built a transformer "blocker" over absolute features of each id and its candidates. The 1st-place author commented that the end-to-end transformer "can be considered to relationship which is not contained in pair training"; it scored single LB 0.918 as a first-stage model — [Foursquare 2nd T0m part](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072)

### Inferences
- The cheapest way to give a pairwise stack "listwise" information is second-stage GBDT features computed over each reference's full candidate list and each target's competing references: rank of this pair's score, gap to the best competitor, number of candidates above threshold, the target's best score from any other reference. This matches what Shopee and Quora winners did and gave +0.001 to +0.003 per feature family there. This is an inference, not a measured transfer.
- A fine-tuned listwise model is most likely to help precisely where one-owner conflicts and near-duplicate distractors dominate the errors. Evidence for a fine-tuned listwise model beating a *strong* fine-tuned pairwise ensemble is thin; the ComEM gains are relative to *zero-shot pairwise* LLMs.

### Gaps
- No Kaggle or SIGMOD winning solution used an end-to-end listwise cross-encoder as the final matcher. No head-to-head of fine-tuned listwise vs fine-tuned pairwise cross-encoders with a GBDT stack was found.
- Exact F1 numbers for Mistral4SelectEM vs Ditto/HierGAT were not retrieved.

## Q4. What gains came from multiple blocking routes (reverse kNN, TF-IDF char n-grams, multiple embedding models) combined with a pruning model?

### Takeaway
Every top Foursquare team unioned at least a geographic route and a name route (TF-IDF or embedding). This raised the recall/IoU ceiling to 0.979–0.994. A cheap GBDT pruner at a very low threshold (0.005–0.01) then removed about 90%+ of candidates while losing only about 0.005–0.008 of ceiling IoU. In the literature, unioning a DL blocker with a rule-based one adds +0.2 to +14.5 absolute recall for up to about 58% more candidates, and TF-IDF 3-gram top-k blocking alone is a surprisingly strong baseline. Fine-tuning the embedding route with its own hard negatives lifted its recall from 0.97 to 0.986.

### Cited Findings
- Foursquare 1st: 100 candidates from lat/lon plus 100 from name embeddings; a light LightGBM kept 20 + 20; max IoU 0.979. A second LightGBM at threshold 0.01 cut candidates to about 10%, with max IoU 0.974 — [1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/re-waiwai-1st-place-solution)
- Foursquare 2nd: 128 candidates from lat/lon plus name TF-IDF with **country-specific mixing ratios**, max IoU 0.9895. A transformer pruner at threshold 0.005 plus union-find gave 4.1 candidates per id at max IoU 0.986 — [2nd place T0m](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336072)
- Foursquare 9th: 350 geo kNN + 50 geo+name-TF-IDF kNN, max IoU 0.994 (420M pairs). A CatBoost pruner on 40 TF-IDF features at threshold 0.005 gave max IoU 0.987 with 3M pairs — [9th place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/foursquare-location-matching/discussion/336415)
- Foursquare 8th: distance kNN (k = 10) + fine-tuned SBERT embedding kNN (k = 20) gave a simulated ideal IoU of 0.986. **Two-stage fine-tuning**, where stage 2 retrained on pairs mined by the stage-1 embedding kNN (hard negatives), raised ideal IoU/recall from 0.97 to 0.986. An English-only model with transliteration (unidecode/pykakasi) beat multilingual models for this route — [8th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/kita-kyushu-lovers-8th-place-solution-sbert-lightg)
- Foursquare 7th: candidates from a combination of haversine 4-NN, 12 regression-based distance/embedding routes, word- and char-level ROUGE similarity routes, and name-cosine top-4 — [7th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/foursquare-location-matching/writeups/in-tokyo-7th-place-solution-with-inference-code)
- Shopee 1st: unioning comb/img/txt match sets (with threshold tuning) gave +0.019 F1 — [Shopee 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/upstage-making-ai-beneficial-1st-place-solution-fr). Shopee 2nd built pairs from the union of the 50 nearest neighbours by image and text similarity — [Shopee 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/lyaka-tkm-2nd-place-solution-matching-prediction-b). Shopee 3rd unioned radius-neighbours across about 7 embeddings and TF-IDFs before a CatBoost pruner/matcher — [Shopee 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/shopee-product-matching/writeups/btbpanda-3rd-place-solution-triplet-loss-boosting-)
- DeepBlocker (VLDB 2021): unioning the best DL blocker with the industrial rule-based RBB raised recall by 0.3–6.7 absolute on structured, 8.5–14.5 on textual and 0.2–9.9 on dirty datasets, while candidate size grew by up to 49.9%, 57.6% and 12.3% respectively — [DeepBlocker paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p2459-thirumuruganathan.pdf)
- Sparkly (VLDB 2023): Lucene TF-IDF top-k blocking with 3-gram tokenization outperformed 8 SOTA blockers, including DL blockers and Union(DL, RBB). Recall was 92.5–100% at k = 10, 96.4–100% at k = 20, and 98.7–100% at k = 50. On Amazon-Google at 98% recall, Sparkly needed a 2.5% candidate ratio vs 10% for the DL blockers — [Sparkly paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p1507-paulsen.pdf)
- SIGMOD 2022 winner: re-ranking embedding kNN candidates by the mean of cosine and Jaccard "increased the generalization of the top-k similarity ranking" — [WBSG poster](https://dbgroup.ing.unimo.it/sigmod22contest/posters/WBSG.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pattern that transfers is a wide union of complementary routes, then a cheap high-recall pruner (GBDT at threshold ≈0.005–0.01), then the expensive matcher on the survivors. The pruner cost about 0.005–0.008 of ceiling IoU in Foursquare while removing about 90–99% of pairs.
- Hard-negative re-mining of the embedding route (8th place: +1.6 points of recall ceiling) and a char-3-gram TF-IDF route (Sparkly) are the cheapest recall adds. Per-country route mixing ratios (2nd place) are directly relevant where country or script behaviour differs.
- Reverse kNN (target → reference) was not named explicitly by any winner. Geographic and name-based kNN run in both directions are implicitly symmetric in a single-table dedup like Foursquare. In a two-table setting, adding the reverse direction is the analogue, but I found no measured gain.
- Changing retrieval changes candidate-dependent features (ranks, density, top-K statistics), so the second stage must be retrained or re-validated. Foursquare 1st did this by re-scoring new pairs after the post-process merge.

### Gaps
- No write-up quantified the end-to-end *final metric* gain (as opposed to the recall/IoU ceiling) from adding a second or third blocking route.
- No explicit reverse-kNN ablation was found in competition write-ups.
